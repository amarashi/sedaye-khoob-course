# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Persian-speaking complete beginners: people who love music but have never read a note and expect theory to be dry, rule-bound homework. Many have already tried once — a book, a class, a YouTube series — and stopped. They arrive in Farsi, right-to-left, usually on a phone, curious but braced for disappointment. The job they want done is to finally understand what they hear well enough to use it on an instrument, a voice, or a keyboard.

No secondary audience is claimed. Everything on the page is written for the beginner.

## Product Purpose

Sedaye Khoob (صدای خوب) is a small Persian music-education studio. This site sells its first product, an online music-theory course, on a single long page that ends in a ZarinPal checkout and an automatically issued Spot Player licence. Success is measured one way: **completed purchases**. Enquiries, traffic, and audience growth matter only insofar as they end in a paid order.

## Positioning

Music theory taught as something you hear, not something you memorise — the only theory course whose first move is to play you the sound and let you recognise it.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: **خرید دوره** — buy the course, checkout on the same page.
- Secondary CTA: **message the studio on WhatsApp**. For visitors who like the course but aren't ready to pay, a human conversation is the fallback, not an email capture.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: **شنیدن قبل از حفظ کردن** ("hearing before memorising").
- Belief ladder — the visitor's blocker is trust in an unfamiliar seller, so the order is:
  1. There are real people behind this, and a real teacher I can see.
  2. This method is not the theory that defeated me before; it starts from sound.
  3. I can tell what I actually get, and roughly what it will feel like to learn here.
  4. Paying is safe, and access is mine to keep.
- Proof on hand: **none yet**. The course is new. The one real asset is the teacher's own background, which the page currently does not show at all — putting it there is the highest-value proof work available. The testimonials in `content/site.json` are placeholders with invented names, and the contact details are `.example` addresses with Australian numbers; neither may be presented as evidence.

## Brand Personality

Warm, alive, unhurried. A loud stage with a calm teacher on it: the design carries the energy — spotlight, colour, movement, the feeling of a room where music is happening — while the writing stays patient and plain, addressing the learner directly and informally. The studio speaks as *ما*, never as a brand. No hype, no urgency tactics, no emoji. A visitor should feel the thrill of music in the first ten seconds and be reassured by the words in the next thirty.

## Anti-references

- Iranian course-selling pages: countdown timers, discount stickers, crossed-out prices, income screenshots, endless testimonial walls.
- Corporate SaaS: generic gradients, tidy feature-card grids, stock office imagery, English-startup polish transplanted into Farsi.
- Classical-music formality: gold-on-black conservatory solemnity, ornate frames, sheet-music wallpaper, maestro grandeur.
- Kids' learning apps: cartoon mascots, primary-colour brightness, gamified badges, cheerful bounce.

## Design Principles

**Loud stage, calm teacher.** Energy belongs to light, colour, motion, and imagery. Copy stays unhurried. When a section feels flat, raise the stage — never the voice.

**Hear it before you read about it.** The course's claim is that theory is audible, so the page should demonstrate rather than assert. Sound, sound-like motion, and the instruments themselves outrank another paragraph of explanation.

**Trust is the conversion problem.** The obstacle is not price or interest, it is an unknown seller. Show the teacher, show the studio, show the real thing being bought. Never manufacture proof — placeholder testimonials cost more credibility than they buy.

**Persian first, not translated.** RTL is the native direction, Estedad is the voice, Persian numerals are used in display contexts. Nothing should read as a Western layout mirrored.

**Illustration carries the meaning.** The rendered "ostad" characters and instrument art are the brand's visual language. Reach for them before reaching for icons, cards, or decorative geometry.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No binding accessibility requirement has been set for this project. As a matter of build quality the existing code already gates its GSAP entrance and scroll reveals behind `prefers-reduced-motion`, and that behaviour should survive future work.
